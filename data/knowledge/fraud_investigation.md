# FinGuard AI fraud investigation knowledge base

## Account takeover
Indicators can include a new or unfamiliar device, abrupt transaction amount changes, unusual access patterns, and activity deviating from the sender's historical baseline. Verify device history and authenticate the account holder using established procedures.

## Unusual large transfer
A transfer materially above a customer's normal amount can warrant review. Compare the amount with historical behavior, account balance and known customer context. A large amount alone is not proof of fraud.

## Rapid repeated transfers
Multiple transfers in a short interval, especially from one sender to multiple receivers or in repeated similar amounts, can indicate structuring or account compromise. Review timestamps, counterparties and the broader transaction sequence.

## Cross-border activity
Unexpected cross-border transfers can merit review when inconsistent with established customer behavior. Confirm geography, customer travel or business context and counterparty legitimacy before escalation.

## Investigation workflow
Treat model flags as triage signals, not conclusions. Corroborate with transaction history, account context and independent evidence. Record evidence, uncertainty and recommended human-review steps. Do not freeze, reject or block payments based on this demo.

## Model limitations
FinGuard AI is trained on synthetic data. Its risk score is not a calibrated real-world probability and must not be used for real financial decisions. Feature importance is global model metadata, not a per-transaction causal explanation.
